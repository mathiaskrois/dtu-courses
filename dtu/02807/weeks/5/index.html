Frequent itemsets

← Week 4Week 5 of 13Week 6 →

Topics

Apriori prunes using downward closure

If an itemset is frequent, every subset is frequent. Use this to reject candidates, but count support in the actual baskets before accepting the remaining ones.

Confidence can be misleading

A rule pointing to an already common item can have high confidence without positive association. Lift compares confidence with the consequent’s baseline frequency.

Thresholds change cost and usefulness

Low support creates more candidates and rules. Explain why the chosen threshold fits the question instead of simply collecting every available association.

Count itemsets and inspect a rule

Optional practice

Baskets: {milk,bread}; {milk,bread,butter}; {bread,butter}; {milk,butter}. Find frequent singles and pairs at minimum support count 2. Calculate confidence and lift for milk → bread.

confidence = count(milk,bread)/count(milk); lift = confidence/P(bread).

Explain whether lift is above or below 1.

Check your answer after trying

Milk, bread and butter each occur in 3 baskets. Every pair occurs in 2. Confidence(milk→bread)=2/3; lift=(2/3)/(3/4)=8/9≈0.889, below 1. The triple occurs once and is not frequent at count 2.

A small Apriori check and a rule interpretation that accounts for its baseline.
