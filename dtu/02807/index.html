Data mining & TF.IDF

Choose meaningful patterns, not coincidences.

Open week 1 page ↗Week page ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. A pattern needs to be useful and valid

    A large dataset produces many apparently interesting coincidences.

  2. TF.IDF combines local frequency and global rarity

    The lecture normalises a term count by the largest term count in that document, then multiplies by log₂(N/df).

  3. Define the question before selecting a tool

    Descriptive and predictive tasks need different evidence.

Read the analysis ↗
02

Practise

30–60 min · Suggested

Calculate a term score and a false-match expectation

Task details

A collection contains 1,024 documents. A term appears in 8 documents, occurs 3 times in this document, and its most frequent term occurs 12 times. Compute TF, IDF and their product. Then consider 10,000 random tests with false-match probability 0.01 each.

YOUR OUTPUT

A checked term score and one sentence about spurious patterns.

WEEK 04 / 22 SEPTEMBER

Similar items & LSH

Understand the similarity measure before approximation.

Open week 4 page ↗Week page ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Jaccard compares sets

    Jaccard similarity is intersection size divided by union size.

  2. Minhash estimates Jaccard similarity

    Matching signature entries estimate a set similarity when hash/permutation assumptions hold.

  3. LSH produces candidates, not final matches

    Banding changes the probability that a pair is considered.

Read the analysis ↗
02

Practise

30–60 min · Suggested

Work through a similarity candidate

Task details

Let A={a,b,c,d}, B={b,c,d,e,f}. Find Jaccard similarity. For an idealised LSH with 20 bands of 5 rows, compute candidate probability 1−(1−s⁵)²⁰. Describe the exact verification step.

YOUR OUTPUT

A similarity calculation and a clear candidate-versus-match distinction.

WEEK 05 / 29 SEPTEMBER

Frequent itemsets

Distinguish frequency, confidence and association.

Open week 5 page ↗Week page ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Apriori prunes using downward closure

    If an itemset is frequent, every subset is frequent.

  2. Confidence can be misleading

    A rule pointing to an already common item can have high confidence without positive association.

  3. Thresholds change cost and usefulness

    Low support creates more candidates and rules.

Read the analysis ↗

WEEK 06 / 6 OCTOBER

Clustering

Compare cluster assumptions and validate the result.

Open week 6 page ↗Week page ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Algorithms encode different cluster shapes

    K-means uses distances to centroids and requires k.

  2. Representation determines the distances

    Feature scales and irrelevant variables can dominate a distance calculation.

  3. One metric is not a complete evaluation

    Davies–Bouldin rewards compact, separated clusters in its distance setting.

Read the analysis ↗

Practise

A checked k-means iteration and a sensible evaluation caveat.

WEEK 07 / 20 OCTOBER

Graph communities

Use structure and a baseline to assess communities.

Open week 7 page ↗Week page ↗
01

Understand

The essential ideas
  1. Betweenness identifies potential bridges

    Edge betweenness counts shortest-path traffic.

  2. Modularity compares observed and expected edges

    A partition’s score compares within-community edges with a degree-based null model.

  3. Compare algorithms on the same graph

    Louvain seeks modularity improvements; spectral approaches use graph matrices and eigenvectors.

Read the analysis ↗
02

Practise

30–60 min · Suggested

Separate two linked triangles

Task details

The undirected graph has edges AB, BC, CA, DE, EF, FD, CD. Split it into {A,B,C} and {D,E,F}. Identify the bridge and compute Q=Σ[Lc/m−(Dc/(2m))²].

YOUR OUTPUT

A community partition and a modularity calculation with explicit counts.
