Requirements & checklist

Submit the report, documented notebook, member contributions and required data. The PDF contains conflicting dates; 4 December is the earlier date on its cover.

  • Use at least one substantial course topic; two are preferred. If using one, make its role crucial and nontrivial, preferably extending it.
  • Include at least one method or technique not directly taught in the lectures, explain it, and show how it contributes.
  • Qualifying course topics: MapReduce, similar items/LSH, frequent itemsets, general clustering, or graph clustering. TF.IDF alone is not a qualifying course topic.
  • Choose a relevant, technically challenging problem; motivate it and justify data, preprocessing, algorithms and design decisions.
  • Evaluate results and whether the approach solves the chosen problem. Package use is allowed, but a trivial wrapper around library calls is insufficient.
  • Report cap: 11,000 characters excluding whitespace for four members; 12,500 for five. Put every member’s name on the title page.
  • Attach a documented Jupyter Notebook with code and sufficient execution instructions, plus an outline of each member’s contribution.
  • Submit all relevant files and runnable data on DTU Learn. If data is too large, the brief allows hosting it on GitHub and including a link.
  • Check the actual submission deadline in Learn: the cover says 4 December 23:59, while the body also mentions 6 December.
