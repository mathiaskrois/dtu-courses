Topics

Circularity is a strategy, not an impact result

Extending use, recovery or recycling can change resource flows. Whether a strategy improves sustainability requires quantifying the resulting changes and trade-offs.

Specify what changes in the system

Identify the material, process or service affected, the recovery rate and any extra energy or processing. A circularity proposal without those assumptions cannot support a defensible comparison.

Readiness affects implementation

The module introduces ready2LOOP and the Circularity Strategy Scanner. Technical feasibility, organisation and other readiness conditions matter when turning a proposed strategy into practice.

Requirements & checklist

Quantify impacts and discuss the balance between the three sustainability pillars.

  • Evaluate environmental, economic and social aspects; discuss trade-offs.
  • Where relevant and possible, calculate the carbon footprint of energy consumption or another relevant impact measure.
  • Put results in perspective using comparable processes or consumables; compare production and use where relevant.
  • One person submits for the whole group in FeedbackFruits.
  • Include a Table of Contributions agreed by every group member.
  • Attach Task 1 as an annex.
  • Default maximum: 9,600 characters (about 4 pages), excluding bibliography and tables; case-specific instructions take precedence.
  • Declare any use of language models. No specific document format is required.
